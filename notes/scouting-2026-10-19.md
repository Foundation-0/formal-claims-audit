# Scouting notes, 19 October 2026

The research gathered for this project during Foundation0's
[deep-fields scouting](https://github.com/Foundation-0/f0/tree/main/deep-fields) of 18–19 October 2026. Facts in the
first section were checked against their sources on 19 Oct 2026. Facts in the second come from the scouting report
and have not been re-checked, so check one before you build on it.

**Question.** When an AI-claimed result is called "Lean-verified" or "formally verified", does its formal statement
say what the paper claims?

**Why it matters.** A proof checker such as Lean confirms that a proof proves its formal statement. It does not
confirm that the formal statement says what the paper claims in words. This project checks that second step, in
public, so that a "formally verified" label means what it says.

**Hub.** [math0](https://github.com/Foundation-0/math0). The project also serves Foundation0's
[Ledger](https://github.com/Foundation-0/f0/blob/main/docs/data/ledger.json).

## Checked on 19 Oct 2026

- **OpenAI's release.** [openai/math](https://github.com/openai/math) was announced on 6 Oct 2026 with 722
  manuscripts in 372 result families
  ([community.openai.com launch post](https://community.openai.com/t/first-look-at-mathematics-manuscripts-from-an-internal-frontier-model-at-openai/1403886)).
  Three were withdrawn on 7 Oct (history.md in that repository), leaving 719. Its README says "~42% top-line results
  formalized", which is 300 of 719. That is OpenAI's own count.
- **A documented mismatch.** Bastounis, Circelli and Hansen, "Navier-Stokes lost in translation: Why Lean
  verification of AI autoformalisation does not guarantee correct natural language proofs",
  [arXiv:2610.08144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08144) (6 Oct 2026): "the formalised Lean proof does not correspond
  to the NL proof of blow-up of solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations." NL means natural language, the proof as
  written in words. Autoformalisation means a machine translating mathematics in words into a formal language.
- **New funding for checks.** On 15 Oct 2026 the Simons Foundation, Anthropic and OpenAI announced $15 million in
  combined funding and resources for workshops and initiatives that help researchers evaluate AI-assisted results,
  use new tools and shape standards ([announcement](https://www.simonsfoundation.org/?p=61382)). Other groups are
  likely to start similar checks.
- **Foundation0's own exposure.** The [Ledger](https://github.com/Foundation-0/f0/blob/main/docs/data/ledger.json)
  has 27 entries whose evidence is "formally-verified" (counted on 19 Oct). Examples:
  `matrix-multiplication-omega-nine-fourths-lean`, `openai-quasi-riemann-hypothesis-7-8`,
  `3sum-apsp-exponents-community-lean`, `exact-dft-lean-record-shea-kothe` and `equational-theories-project`.

  ([Pebblous audit](https://blog.pebblous.ai/report/openai-math-722-verification-ledger/en/)).
- **The 23 Erdős results.** Thomas Bloom reportedly said he had not checked that the Lean files compile or that the
  formal statements capture the problems. The Lean file for Problem 3 proves only a weaker bound
  ([OfficeChai, 9 Oct](https://officechai.com/ai/openai-has-also-included-proofs-for-23-erdos-problems-in-its-latest-math-proof-drop/),
  a secondary source).
- **formal-conjectures.** Google DeepMind's formal-conjectures
  [pull request #6898](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/pull/6898), merged on 11 Oct, linked
  about 25 to 29 openai/math results. It flagged gaps between statements for Borsuk, Green 42, Erdős 371, 952 and
  1150, and circulant Hadamard.
- **Others checking, and what they don't do.**
  - [Big O Speedrun](https://bigospeedrun.com/about) runs Lean's Comparator against fixed statements for 20
    problems, but does not compare them with the papers.
  - The [Palomar registry](https://palomar-registry.org/about) (Lean FRO and ICARM) registers only what authors
    submit, and uses a language model to judge whether statements are faithful.
  - queelius/openai-math-counterexamples re-computes the 63 counterexample families: 8 hold, 0 fail, and the rest are
    not explicit or not finite.
  - No systematic audit across labs of whether formal statements match their papers was found.
- **Tools.**
  - Lean's [Comparator](https://github.com/leanprover/comparator), which needs Lean 4.35 or later.
  - nanoda, a second Lean kernel: an independent program that re-checks Lean proofs.
  - `#print axioms`, which lists the axioms a proof depends on.
- **Foundation0 work that depends on this.** Foundation0 members already build on openai/math families 107, 130,
  160, 161 and 167 ([machine mathematics](https://fdn0.org/research/machine-mathematics/)).

## Method proposed during scouting

1. Pin each source at a commit.
2. Build the proof and record the axioms it uses.
3. Take an independent statement of the same claim: from formal-conjectures, or written from the paper without
   looking at the lab's version.
4. Compare the two with Comparator or a short bridge lemma, a Lean proof that one statement implies the other.
5. Give a verdict: faithful, weaker, stronger, different, or does not build. One file per claim under
   [verdicts/](../verdicts/README.md), with the commit, both statements and the command output.
6. A person who knows the field reviews every verdict other than "faithful" before it is reported.

Frame findings as "the formal statement differs from the paper's claim", never "the proof is wrong". An independent
statement can itself be wrong (Erdős #124's formal statement once had a typo), so record who wrote it.
